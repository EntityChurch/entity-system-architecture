| **`EXPLORATION-THE-INTERACTION-LANDSCAPE-WHAT-FITS-WHAT-DOES-NOT-AND-WHERE-THE-BOUNDARY-IS`** | **Read before telling anyone what this substrate is for — its most useful output is the list of things it should NOT be used for.** One distinction predicts every verdict in it: **is the state OWNED AND PUBLISHED, or SIMULATED AND CONTENDED?** Local-view authority — each peer is the authority for its own namespace — *is* a statement that there is no shared mutable state, which is the property making everything else cheap; and **the boundary does not run between social and games, it runs through the middle of a game** (a player's inventory is owned, the world's physics is contended). **Games split about half and half, and some of the fitting half fits BETTER than the social case**: mods and assets (a content-addressed store with dedup is exactly what an asset pipeline wants — identical textures stored once, integrity free, CDN-cacheable forever because the hash is the name, *"do I have this?"* is a hash comparison, and **the existing platforms are re-implementing content addressing badly**) and replays (an immutable signed artefact with a permanent verifiable identity on cheap static hosting). Inventory is the model's home ground because **ownership is a signed claim in your own namespace**; leaderboards are a mirror; guild rosters are a collection; trade is a grant. **What does not fit and must not be made to**: authoritative world simulation, real-time position and physics, ephemeral tick state, matchmaking, and **anti-cheat — which generalizes furthest and is worth one sentence: a signature proves authorship, never truth.** A player can validly sign *"I scored 4,000,000"*; adjudication needs an authority that holds the rules. **The shape that would serve games already has a name** — the *live* rung of the static/hosted/live dial plus a capability, whose one irreducible power is **evaluating a grant at fetch time**; so publish what players own, run a live peer for what the world adjudicates, and the halves interoperate because they are one substrate at two rungs. **Asynchronous games need no live rung at all** and are an underserved category this serves unusually well. **§5 is the structural-impossibility list to publish rather than let people discover**: no global uniqueness or scarcity (no gatekeeper means nobody decides who was first — what exists is **issuer-scoped** uniqueness, which is real and is not the same thing) · **no global counts** (you cannot count what you cannot enumerate; what is honest is a **per-reader** count that varies between readers, which removes engagement metrics as a manipulation surface and **is probably the single biggest product risk in the design**) · no enforced deletion or expiry · **no sybil resistance** (identity is free; the real but narrow mitigation is that nothing is pushed and every route is opt-in, so spam needs a route — genuinely stronger than an open inbox, and it fails exactly where openness is the point, which is the forum) · and key-loss recovery, **the failure a normal user is most likely to actually hit**. Also: **collaborative editing is the one contended case with a real answer** (CRDTs) and is a separate convention rather than a fifth shape, with **publication as the seam**; and **an event needs no new type** — an RSVP is an entry in the attendee's namespace with a `context`, assembled by the host as a mirror — while the thing an event platform sells, a guaranteed-complete guest list and a hard capacity cap, is a contended scarce resource and needs the live rung. **Sourcing is weaker than the rest of the corpus and says so**: games, marketplaces and collaborative editors are products with no specification to read, so §2 is reasoned from observable behaviour and is a hypothesis a game developer could refute in a conversation |
| **`EXPLORATION-THE-L5-CONTENT-TAXONOMY-AND-THE-FLOOR-THAT-STOPS-THE-EXPLOSION`** ⭐ | **Read this before proposing ANY new application entity type — it is the anti-explosion gate, and it exists because a vocabulary fails in two directions at once.** Supplies **the test**: *two things are different entity types if and only if a conformant consumer holding one must BEHAVE differently than if it held the other* — not if they are called different things, look different, or would sit in different tabs. **Three corollaries kill three categories of would-be type**: different body ⇒ same type (dispatch absorbs it, so **a vlog is not a type**), different presentation ⇒ same type (charter #2 already forbids it in the format), and **different discovery ⇒ same type**, which is the least obvious and does the most work. The inverse is the guard against over-unifying: collapsing two genuinely different consumer obligations into one type with a mode field **pushes the distinction into a value**, where nothing checks the branch — and a wrong mode value is invisible where a wrong type tag at least fails a filter. **The landscape half is the operator's fear observed rather than argued**: the large-vocabulary lineage ships a broad object and activity vocabulary and **deployed interop is a small subset of it**, so a large vocabulary does not produce large interoperability — it produces a de-facto small one plus a long tail handled differently everywhere; the small-vocabulary lineage keeps few record types **and writes down the rules for growing them**, and we currently have neither half. **The result: six of ten named products collapse into one type**, and collections type by **provenance rather than subject** — *who assembled this list and what does it therefore claim* has exactly three answers (**the author's unbounded cursored stream** · **the author's bounded complete authored-order set** · **a reader's gathered view that claims nothing**), so the taxonomy terminates instead of running to a type per product. **Two surprising placements carry the document.** *A forum needs no new entity type at all* — a forum post is an entry with `reply` and `context`, a thread is a gathered view, and the only thing distinguishing a forum from a comment section is **how you arrived**, which is discovery; what the forum actually needs is a subject nobody owns (a topic that is a content hash) plus navigation, and **no entity type solves navigation**. *A chat message is an entry and a conversation is not a collection* — the message shares every field, while the conversation has a roster, expects delivery, is readable by a membership, and treats a missing item as a bug rather than a short view, so **the conversation earns machinery and the message does not earn a type**; if they stay separate **every crossing between a feed, a forum and a conversation needs a mapping, and mappings are where vocabularies fork.** Also settles **deletion**: withdrawal is **unpublication, not erasure** — removal stops your origin serving it and touches nobody's held copy, there is **no global takedown ever**, and **a tombstone is a promise this architecture cannot keep** because the holder set is unbounded and uncoordinated, so the honest sentence belongs in the UI at the moment of the action. **§8 lists five things it does not settle**, including the most likely fifth type (**a profile**, which nobody asked for and every surveyed system has) |
| **`EXPLORATION-WHAT-CONVERGES-WHAT-CANNOT-AND-THE-ORDER-TO-BUILD-IT`** ⭐ | **The capstone — read this one if you read one.** States the system in one page, then does the three things the derivations did not. **(a) Separates four use cases into three difficulty tiers** — *publish a feed and follow feeds* is **easy and not in doubt** (no shared space, nothing to reconcile, and *"I know exactly where to look because you told me where you are"*), *reply and thread* is moderate, **a forum is hard for one specific reason**, encrypted groups are hardest — *"treating them as one project is what makes the whole thing look intractable."* **(b) Separates three kinds of convergence and shows only one is a correctness requirement:** **set** (do we hold the same entries — must converge), **order** (must converge only where cheap), **selection** (**must NOT converge — divergence is the feature**, since a system where selection converged would be one with a central moderator). **(c) The forum result, and it is exact rather than metaphorical:** a thread is a **grow-only set of immutable, content-addressed, signed entries**, so merging views is **set union** — idempotent, commutative, associative, and **verifiable in a way ordinary convergent structures are not, because every element carries its author's signature and no replica can inject a fake one.** Completeness is therefore **unattainable in principle and monotonically repairable in practice**: *you never have a wrong view, only a short one* — where a centralized forum can show you a view that is **wrong** (ranked against you, quietly filtered) this can only show you one that is **incomplete**, and incompleteness is visible by comparing mirrors and fixable by meeting more people. **Activity produces redundancy; redundancy produces completeness**, and two subnetworks that discussed the same thing independently **merge with no work at all.** So **aggregation is the solved part and NAVIGATION is what is missing** — lazy traversal under a budget, a retention decision, and *following ideas rather than people*, for which **a topic that is a content hash gives a global name anyone can compute and nobody owns.** **(d) The interpretation problem answered without centralizing it:** chronological and reply order converge for free, so the 90% needs no machinery; **ranking is an opinion and the architecture already knows how to handle opinions — publish them**, subscribable like block lists, several coexisting, nobody having to win — and **where auditability is wanted the ranking function is a content-addressed program, so "we shipped a new version" is a different hash rather than a silent behaviour change and version skew becomes a content-addressing problem.** **(e) Scale:** the pull precedent held for 25 years and its failures were **always client-side**; our check is cheaper (compare a signed root, ~10 KB, unchanged ⇒ no work) but the **linear term is unchanged**, so a hundred thousand follows is the mirror tier pointed at fan-in — which costs completeness, never integrity. §7 gives the **four-stage build order whose first artifact is demonstrable in a sentence**, and §8 lists six things genuinely unknown, including the honest one: **whether people adopt one vocabulary is a social question no architecture can force** |
| `EXPLORATION-THE-RESOLUTION-CHAIN-FROM-A-SOCIAL-IDENTIFIER-TO-AN-ENTITY` | **Read this before any work on feeds, posts, following, or bridging to an outside social protocol — it is the only document that traces the whole path at once.** Rates **five links** by what exists rather than what is intended: identifier → peer (**designed, one hole**) · peer → tree (**deployed**) · **the content model (EMPTY)** · the follow loop (**designed, unbuilt**) · the bridge (**framed, never carried forward**). **The keystone finding: link 3 is empty and sits between two finished links** — we can resolve a name and pull a signed tree and have no vocabulary for what to put in it, so every downstream item (feed, follow UI, feed emission, every bridge) has nothing to be *about*. Establishes that the content-site convention **already deferred semantic feeds on a named trigger** — *"authored when a concrete feed use case arrives"*, plus its own *"there is something here we haven't fully found"* — and that **the trigger has now fired**. Supplies the **ninth prior-art axis** the source library lacks (it has eight, none of them the data model — which is *why* the empty link never surfaced): the ActivityPub/WebFinger and ATProto/DID resolution chains read from primary specs, the ActivityStreams-object vs Lexicon-record comparison, and **Lexicon's compatibility rule** (*"all old data must still be valid under the updated Lexicon, and new data must be valid under the old"*) — of which **we hold the wire half and not the vocabulary half**. ## §9 Keeping this index true

- **Add a row in the same commit that adds the document.** An index that lags is worse than none.
- **`spec ledger` gates the counts** against the directories — same rung that already holds
  `docs/proposals/INDEX.md`.
- **A row states what the document *establishes*, not what it is about.** "Relay prior art" is not a
  row; "why the four modes are a design space and not a taxonomy to prune" is.
- **Struck-through or retracted content stays listed, marked** — a reader must be able to find a
  superseded conclusion in order to know it was superseded.
