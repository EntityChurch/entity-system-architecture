| **`EXPLORATION-THE-INTERACTION-LANDSCAPE-WHAT-FITS-WHAT-DOES-NOT-AND-WHERE-THE-BOUNDARY-IS`** | **Read before telling anyone what this substrate is for — its most useful output is the list of things it should NOT be used for.** One distinction predicts every verdict in it: **is the state OWNED AND PUBLISHED, or SIMULATED AND CONTENDED?** Local-view authority — each peer is the authority for its own namespace — *is* a statement that there is no shared mutable state, which is the property making everything else cheap; and **the boundary does not run between social and games, it runs through the middle of a game** (a player's inventory is owned, the world's physics is contended). **Games split about half and half, and some of the fitting half fits BETTER than the social case**: mods and assets (a content-addressed store with dedup is exactly what an asset pipeline wants — identical textures stored once, integrity free, CDN-cacheable forever because the hash is the name, *"do I have this?"* is a hash comparison, and **the existing platforms are re-implementing content addressing badly**) and replays (an immutable signed artefact with a permanent verifiable identity on cheap static hosting). Inventory is the model's home ground because **ownership is a signed claim in your own namespace**; leaderboards are a mirror; guild rosters are a collection; trade is a grant. **What does not fit and must not be made to**: authoritative world simulation, real-time position and physics, ephemeral tick state, matchmaking, and **anti-cheat — which generalizes furthest and is worth one sentence: a signature proves authorship, never truth.** A player can validly sign *"I scored 4,000,000"*; adjudication needs an authority that holds the rules. **The shape that would serve games already has a name** — the *live* rung of the static/hosted/live dial plus a capability, whose one irreducible power is **evaluating a grant at fetch time**; so publish what players own, run a live peer for what the world adjudicates, and the halves interoperate because they are one substrate at two rungs. **Asynchronous games need no live rung at all** and are an underserved category this serves unusually well. **§5 is the structural-impossibility list to publish rather than let people discover**: no global uniqueness or scarcity (no gatekeeper means nobody decides who was first — what exists is **issuer-scoped** uniqueness, which is real and is not the same thing) · **no global counts** (you cannot count what you cannot enumerate; what is honest is a **per-reader** count that varies between readers, which removes engagement metrics as a manipulation surface and **is probably the single biggest product risk in the design**) · no enforced deletion or expiry · **no sybil resistance** (identity is free; the real but narrow mitigation is that nothing is pushed and every route is opt-in, so spam needs a route — genuinely stronger than an open inbox, and it fails exactly where openness is the point, which is the forum) · and key-loss recovery, **the failure a normal user is most likely to actually hit**. Also: **collaborative editing is the one contended case with a real answer** (CRDTs) and is a separate convention rather than a fifth shape, with **publication as the seam**; and **an event needs no new type** — an RSVP is an entry in the attendee's namespace with a `context`, assembled by the host as a mirror — while the thing an event platform sells, a guaranteed-complete guest list and a hard capacity cap, is a contended scarce resource and needs the live rung. **Sourcing is weaker than the rest of the corpus and says so**: games, marketplaces and collaborative editors are products with no specification to read, so §2 is reasoned from observable behaviour and is a hypothesis a game developer could refute in a conversation |
| **`EXPLORATION-THE-L5-CONTENT-TAXONOMY-AND-THE-FLOOR-THAT-STOPS-THE-EXPLOSION`** ⭐ | **Read this before proposing ANY new application entity type — it is the anti-explosion gate, and it exists because a vocabulary fails in two directions at once.** Supplies **the test**: *two things are different entity types if and only if a conformant consumer holding one must BEHAVE differently than if it held the other* — not if they are called different things, look different, or would sit in different tabs. **Three corollaries kill three categories of would-be type**: different body ⇒ same type (dispatch absorbs it, so **a vlog is not a type**), different presentation ⇒ same type (charter #2 already forbids it in the format), and **different discovery ⇒ same type**, which is the least obvious and does the most work. The inverse is the guard against over-unifying: collapsing two genuinely different consumer obligations into one type with a mode field **pushes the distinction into a value**, where nothing checks the branch — and a wrong mode value is invisible where a wrong type tag at least fails a filter. **The landscape half is the operator's fear observed rather than argued**: the large-vocabulary lineage ships a broad object and activity vocabulary and **deployed interop is a small subset of it**, so a large vocabulary does not produce large interoperability — it produces a de-facto small one plus a long tail handled differently everywhere; the small-vocabulary lineage keeps few record types **and writes down the rules for growing them**, and we currently have neither half. **The result: six of ten named products collapse into one type**, and collections type by **provenance rather than subject** — *who assembled this list and what does it therefore claim* has exactly three answers (**the author's unbounded cursored stream** · **the author's bounded complete authored-order set** · **a reader's gathered view that claims nothing**), so the taxonomy terminates instead of running to a type per product. **Two surprising placements carry the document.** *A forum needs no new entity type at all* — a forum post is an entry with `reply` and `context`, a thread is a gathered view, and the only thing distinguishing a forum from a comment section is **how you arrived**, which is discovery; what the forum actually needs is a subject nobody owns (a topic that is a content hash) plus navigation, and **no entity type solves navigation**. *A chat message is an entry and a conversation is not a collection* — the message shares every field, while the conversation has a roster, expects delivery, is readable by a membership, and treats a missing item as a bug rather than a short view, so **the conversation earns machinery and the message does not earn a type**; if they stay separate **every crossing between a feed, a forum and a conversation needs a mapping, and mappings are where vocabularies fork.** Also settles **deletion**: withdrawal is **unpublication, not erasure** — removal stops your origin serving it and touches nobody's held copy, there is **no global takedown ever**, and **a tombstone is a promise this architecture cannot keep** because the holder set is unbounded and uncoordinated, so the honest sentence belongs in the UI at the moment of the action. **§8 lists five things it does not settle**, including the most likely fifth type (**a profile**, which nobody asked for and every surveyed system has) |
| **`EXPLORATION-WHAT-CONVERGES-WHAT-CANNOT-AND-THE-ORDER-TO-BUILD-IT`** ⭐ | **The capstone — read this one if you read one.** States the system in one page, then does the three things the derivations did not. **(a) Separates four use cases into three difficulty tiers** — *publish a feed and follow feeds* is **easy and not in doubt** (no shared space, nothing to reconcile, and *"I know exactly where to look because you told me where you are"*), *reply and thread* is moderate, **a forum is hard for one specific reason**, encrypted groups are hardest — *"treating them as one project is what makes the whole thing look intractable."* **(b) Separates three kinds of convergence and shows only one is a correctness requirement:** **set** (do we hold the same entries — must converge), **order** (must converge only where cheap), **selection** (**must NOT converge — divergence is the feature**, since a system where selection converged would be one with a central moderator). **(c) The forum result, and it is exact rather than metaphorical:** a thread is a **grow-only set of immutable, content-addressed, signed entries**, so merging views is **set union** — idempotent, commutative, associative, and **verifiable in a way ordinary convergent structures are not, because every element carries its author's signature and no replica can inject a fake one.** Completeness is therefore **unattainable in principle and monotonically repairable in practice**: *you never have a wrong view, only a short one* — where a centralized forum can show you a view that is **wrong** (ranked against you, quietly filtered) this can only show you one that is **incomplete**, and incompleteness is visible by comparing mirrors and fixable by meeting more people. **Activity produces redundancy; redundancy produces completeness**, and two subnetworks that discussed the same thing independently **merge with no work at all.** So **aggregation is the solved part and NAVIGATION is what is missing** — lazy traversal under a budget, a retention decision, and *following ideas rather than people*, for which **a topic that is a content hash gives a global name anyone can compute and nobody owns.** **(d) The interpretation problem answered without centralizing it:** chronological and reply order converge for free, so the 90% needs no machinery; **ranking is an opinion and the architecture already knows how to handle opinions — publish them**, subscribable like block lists, several coexisting, nobody having to win — and **where auditability is wanted the ranking function is a content-addressed program, so "we shipped a new version" is a different hash rather than a silent behaviour change and version skew becomes a content-addressing problem.** **(e) Scale:** the pull precedent held for 25 years and its failures were **always client-side**; our check is cheaper (compare a signed root, ~10 KB, unchanged ⇒ no work) but the **linear term is unchanged**, so a hundred thousand follows is the mirror tier pointed at fan-in — which costs completeness, never integrity. §7 gives the **four-stage build order whose first artifact is demonstrable in a sentence**, and §8 lists six things genuinely unknown, including the honest one: **whether people adopt one vocabulary is a social question no architecture can force** |
| `EXPLORATION-THE-RESOLUTION-CHAIN-FROM-A-SOCIAL-IDENTIFIER-TO-AN-ENTITY` | **Read this before any work on feeds, posts, following, or bridging to an outside social protocol — it is the only document that traces the whole path at once.** Rates **five links** by what exists rather than what is intended: identifier → peer (**designed, one hole**) · peer → tree (**deployed**) · **the content model (EMPTY)** · the follow loop (**designed, unbuilt**) · the bridge (**framed, never carried forward**). **The keystone finding: link 3 is empty and sits between two finished links** — we can resolve a name and pull a signed tree and have no vocabulary for what to put in it, so every downstream item (feed, follow UI, feed emission, every bridge) has nothing to be *about*. Establishes that the content-site convention **already deferred semantic feeds on a named trigger** — *"authored when a concrete feed use case arrives"*, plus its own *"there is something here we haven't fully found"* — and that **the trigger has now fired**. Supplies the **ninth prior-art axis** the source library lacks (it has eight, none of them the data model — which is *why* the empty link never surfaced): the ActivityPub/WebFinger and ATProto/DID resolution chains read from primary specs, the ActivityStreams-object vs Lexicon-record comparison, and **Lexicon's compatibility rule** (*"all old data must still be valid under the updated Lexicon, and new data must be valid under the old"*) — of which **we hold the wire half and not the vocabulary half**. Two rules to steal, both independently confirmed by our own defects: **bidirectional name verification** (ATProto refuses to trust a handle until the document links back — the same hazard as a resolver that checks a binding's signature but not that it is *for the name asked*) and a **stated compatibility contract with conformance artifacts** for any published vocabulary. Also carries: why **public following of a stranger is the static route only** (no grant), the measurement that makes an **ordered feed index a prerequisite rather than a feature** (verifying a known region ≈ tree depth; discovering what is new under a prefix costs the whole tree), and **the honest asymmetry** — we hold the hard halves (content addressing, canonical Merkle, signed pointers, keys-not-servers, fine-grained authority) and lack the easy one (*a record with an author, a timestamp, a body, and a place in an order*), which is why the gap is invisible from inside |

| `EXPLORATION-THE-KEY-AS-HUB-NAMING-TOPOLOGY-AND-THE-REPUBLICATION-NETWORK` | **Read with the chain audit — that one finds the gap, this one gives the shape underneath it.** Establishes **naming as a hub topology, not a chain**: many independent inbound routes (DNS record · well-known path · issued name · petname · typed key · QR · rendezvous · a link · a reference in someone's data) converge on **one self-certifying key**, the route is discardable once it terminates, and trust is a per-route property — so *"is there one canonical naming form"* is answered **no, and it is a design error to want one**; the naming layer can grow forever without the data layer learning about it. Splits **the reverse direction into two questions nobody had separated**: **R1** key → endpoint (the known hole) and **R2** key → *what names does it claim* — and shows the naive reverse index is the wrong shape (needs an authority, unverifiable, enumeration leak) where the deployed answer inverts it: **the key publishes its claimed names and the consumer verifies each forward**, which is the same bidirectional rule one major protocol makes normative and which our own resolver defect independently confirms. **Recommends one self-published signed record carrying endpoints + claimed names + services + `seq` + `supersedes`**, since the questions share a signer, a lifetime and a freshness rule — with the warning that a positionally-authoritative record becomes forgeable the moment it travels. Carries the **survival matrix** — domain loss costs *a label, not an identity*; host loss costs nothing; **key loss is the only fatal one** — so *"migrate your followers"* is free in two of three rows and the honest open problem is key change, where the natural fix (a successor signed by the old key) is exactly what a **compromised** key must not be able to assert. Reduces **ten social products to six content shapes** (Entry · Reference · Collection · Reaction · Relation · **Context**) stable across thirty years, with the warning that **Context and audience are orthogonal and collapsing them is the expensive mistake**. Pins the **reference shape** — `{peer, path, hash}` with root and parent — from the strong-reference and mail-ancestry lineages, giving a reply that is **verifiable without contacting the party referenced**. §6 works out **the republication network**: participation *is* publishing, so thread assembly is a by-product rather than a role, which **dissolves the aggregator-centralization problem** (an aggregator can omit but never substitute) and makes **discovery fall out of the reference graph** — with the costs stated plainly: unknowable completeness, retention debt, no edit/delete propagation, **no moderation axis anywhere in this corpus**, and replies as unsolicited republication |

| `EXPLORATION-THE-PUBLIC-SOCIAL-STACK-THE-COMPLETE-MAP` | **The assembled map — start here for the social tier, and read the other two only for the derivations.** Public case only, deliberately; confidential entries, session encryption and membership'd conversations are scoped out in §8 with what is already known about each. **Rates the stack in eight layers and the result is unusual: 0–2 (identity · naming · distribution) are done and are the hard part, 3 is designed, and 4–6 (content · assembly · curation) do not exist and are the only layers a user ever sees.** Answers **the seven questions any social system must answer**, showing the infrastructure column filled and the social column empty — and that **question 5 is where the model is structurally different**: every other system's publisher does work proportional to follower count, ours writes to its own tree and stops, so a million followers and one cost the same. Works out **four shapes in proposable detail** — the entry (body is an embed node, `author` MUST equal the namespace, which is the anti-forgery rule), the **reference** `{peer, path, hash}` with root+parent threading (derived from the split between the location-only and location-plus-hash lineages), the **paged index** — a bounded mutable head page with an immutable content-addressed back-chain, which satisfies the site convention's no-collection ruling rather than reopening it and turns the measured whole-tree walk into **O(new)** — and the **mirror**, with four rules of which *republish the original bytes* and *a mirror is not authorship* are what make republication structurally not-forgery. **Closes the two axes the corpus never had.** §5 **moderation**: nobody can remove anything from anyone's tree, so the question is what a participant controls — fetch, render, **republish** — and since *a mirror can omit but never substitute*, **omission is the native operation and blocking is the default capability rather than a bolted-on feature**; readers of my mirrors inherit my exclusions visibly, **curation and moderation are the same act**, following is already subscribing to someone's moderation, shared block lists are ordinary data — with the hard limits stated: exclusion is not removal, no global takedown for anything ever, and no platform to appeal to as the honest counterpart of no platform. §6 **economics**: our always-on tier is a static website, so it falls on the surviving side of the library's own finding (*a tier also useful for something else survives; one that exists only to serve the network is a donation with a half-life*) — **a domain is optional** (the key is the identity), there is **no per-user server cost and no per-follower cost**, cost scales with what you publish rather than who reads, moving hosts changes no hash, and **a well-discussed thread outlives its author's host** because mirrors carry it. §7 states the thesis with four limits including *we have no network effects and a better architecture does not move people — it lowers the cost of leaving* |

| `EXPLORATION-THE-CONVERGENCE-THESIS-THREE-LAYERS-AND-THE-HOSTED-TIER` | **Read for *why the stack composes*, and before answering "could we also do X on this?"** States the thesis — *a network is a group who agreed on a data model and the code that reads it; once that agreement is verifiable, following/threading/moderation/hosting/discovery are consequences rather than features* — with its inverse as the standing risk: **an unverified agreement is indistinguishable from agreement until it matters.** Separates **three independent convergences, each with its own hash-based verification and each rated by rung**: **implementation** (generated peers against a conformance oracle — cross-verified for the core, and *nothing above core is generated because the generator's input scope is its output scope*), **data** (the entity vocabulary, checked by vectors — cross-verified for core types, **specified-only for the app tier, where all three landed conventions ship zero artifacts and one has measurably diverged**), and **compute** (the IR plus the descriptor's `(role, shape)` driver ABI — *"a program = descriptor + step IR + initial_state, all content-addressed; transfer those three by hash and any conformant host runs it"*). **Confirms compute convergence is the functional twin of data convergence and that the design already exists** — data asks *do two impls produce the same bytes for this entity*, compute asks *do two engines produce the same boundary hash for this evaluation* — while recommending it stay out of the social proposal, since the track is deliberately sequenced behind the reachability work. §3 works out **the hosted publishing tier**: verify a signature, write to static storage, no read path, no per-tenant runtime — so **the trust surface is availability and privacy only, never integrity or identity** (a host can refuse, withhold, observe and correlate; it cannot forge, alter, impersonate, or take your followers), **exit is free by construction**, and the marginal cost per tenant is bytes plus one signature check, which is why the economics survive popularity where a federated instance's invert under it. Pins the design rule that keeps it honest: **a hosted service MUST NOT be able to produce any entity a self-hoster could not produce identically.** §4 frames **static/hosted/live as one cost-liveness dial on one system** — same identity, tree and followers across all three — with the one thing static genuinely cannot do (request-time authority, which is why confidential entries are not interchangeable with audience-scoping). §5 checks the *"this generalizes to repos and build systems"* claim rather than asserting it: the version DAG with common-ancestor finding and divergence detection is specified, the social work just supplied issues/review/forks, and **the one genuinely absent forge primitive is refs and branches over the DAG** — with three things that do **not** come free named outright: **search** (no design, no owner, and no reason to expect it to fall out of anything), the legal/abuse surface, and network effects |

| Document | What it establishes |

| `EXPLORATION-THE-HYPERTEXT-LINEAGE-XANADU-HYPER-G-AND-WHAT-THE-WEB-DECLINED-TO-SOLVE` | **The dead designs, which are more useful than the living ones because they have a recorded diagnosis.** The headline result is checkable and not a moral about perfectionism: **every part of Xanadu never implemented — in any release, across forty years and three codebases — is a part that required a globally coordinated authority** (tumbler allocation, *"every text exists only as an original"*, and rule 9's royalty mechanism, which is absent from Green, Gold **and** OpenXanadu); **every part implemented is a part that was local.** We have replaced each global requirement with a local one — content hash for allocated address, redundancy-as-mechanism for no-redundancy, detached signature for royalty accounting. **Nelson's own framing is worth taking twice:** links are ***applicative*** — *"applying from outside to content which is already in place with stable addresses"*, permitting *"thousands of overlapping links… created without coordination"* — which is a better statement of why our references live in the referring party's tree than anything in our corpus; and the **content list / EDL** (*"delivery is in two logical phases: the content list, then the fulfilment of the contents"*) is our mirror and collection, independently derived three times counting Hollywood. **The map of their five properties against ours** finds us strictly ahead on addressing (a content hash is the primitive they needed and could not have) and on attribution (a signature travels with the bytes; royalty settlement never shipped), level on versioning, ahead on deletion — **and one gap: no span-level addressing.** That is the finding: the hardest problem in the whole lineage — making a sub-document address survive an edit, which cost Xanadu enfilades and costs Hypothes.is a fuzzy-match chain over `TextQuoteSelector` + `TextPositionSelector` + Bitap — **is arithmetic in this substrate, because our entries are immutable.** `{reference, offset, length}`, exact and permanent. Not proposed, and nobody has asked. **`Hyper-G` is the more instructive failure and gets its own section**: someone *did* ship bidirectional links, third-party annotation on read-only documents, guaranteed link integrity (delete a document and links to it vanish network-wide; restore it and they reappear) and `p-flood` for referential integrity across thousands of servers — **and lost anyway**, because the guarantees held only inside its own world, the client talked to one server per session, interoperating meant conceding, and by 2000 the differentiators were Apache modules. ***p-flood was an elegant solution to a problem the Web won by declining to solve*** — and that sentence is the transferable rule: **before building a mechanism, ask whether the property is one the competing design lives without, and whether it survives at the edge of your own deployment.** With Berners-Lee's own framing of the trade (*"had to throw away the ideal of total consistency… but allowing unchecked exponential uncontrolled growth"*), scored honestly in both directions |
| `EXPLORATION-THE-CONVERGENT-DESIGNS-WILLOW-SSB-USENET-MATRIX-AND-THE-FIVE-VERDICTS` | **The living and operationally-retired systems — four of `REFERENCE-PRIOR-ART-BY-AXIS` §9's five named gaps closed, plus one it did not know to name.** **`Willow` is our nearest living neighbour by a wide margin** and the only system whose authors published a point-by-point rationale against every other: five independent convergences on our load-bearing choices (**naming over pure content-addressing**; continuous binding to an agreed name; ***"deliberately avoids any cryptographic proofs of completeness, allowing for traceless data removal"*** — our trie result, reached separately; capabilities over public keys; and ActivityPub's server-bound names as *"brittleness"*). Their content-addressing sentence reads like an indictment and is not — *"pure content-addressing faces a bootstrapping problem: I must first inform them about the hash"* is a diagnosis of IPFS, and **our `path → hash` trie under a signed root is their answer, at the layer they say is missing** — so we sit between IPFS and Willow and are the only one of the three with both self-verifying content and traceless removal. **They also supply a reason for `prev` being opt-in that we never wrote down: a mandatory single-writer chain *"precludes concurrent writes from multiple devices"*** — one human with two devices. **`SSB` is the natural experiment**: mandatory chain, and the costs are in their own docs — whole-feed replication as *"a major friction point for onboarding"* (partial replication retrofitted), deletion structurally impossible, forked feeds unresolved, the transitive-interest-graph problem, and the ICN paper's admission that *"pseudonymity, non-refutability and immutability can be a serious risk"* and participation *"currently favors privileged users."* **Their sliced-replication hazard generalizes and we have the same class already recorded**: *partial replication is not free — you must preserve the authorization skeleton when you prune content.* **`Usenet` is the only real answer to retention**: flood-fill plus `Message-ID` dedup plus `ihave`/`sendme` (which is set reconciliation, invented operationally in the 1980s, and a content hash is a `Message-ID` you cannot forge and need not allocate) — and full replication was cheap until 5 TB/day became 9 TB/day, at which point **retention stopped being a policy and became a price**, 120 days (2007) → 900 (2011) → 5,000+ today, 25 PB for 2,000 days. **`Matrix` is the price of contended mutable state**: state resolution v1 had state resets exploitable to revert room state, v2 *"was a bit of a mission"* in 2018, and **v2.1 landed in room version 12 in 2025** — seven years of repairs, still going. We have no state resolution **because we never made a shared object mutable**, and Matrix is the map of what Stage 4 costs if we get membership wrong. **`BitTorrent` endorses capstone §1**: it is a stack, not a progression — **PEX structurally cannot bootstrap**, obtains only 30–40% of peers immediately and under 60% ever, and private swarms disable the DHT for lack of authentication, so **no layer won and the always-on route cannot be removed.** §7 answers the pocketed bloom-filter question well enough to file: the technique is **range-based set reconciliation** (Meyer, SRDS 2023), it has **no false positives** where Bloom/IBLT do, its local work is proportional to the *difference* rather than the set, and it requires **a totally ordered set with range-summarizable fingerprints — which our canonical Merkle trie already is.** Deployed twice (Willow's `range-reconcile`, Nostr's Negentropy). **The item is no longer *"do bloom filters help"* but *"measure RBSR against the trie we already have."*** Also separates the operator's Chrome Safe Browsing parallel as a *different* problem — asymmetric membership testing, where a Bloom filter is right and ours is the symmetric case where it is not |
| `EXPLORATION-EVOLVABLE-SCHEMAS-AND-THE-VOCABULARY-PROBLEM` | **Operator-requested, and its first move is the decomposition the field already has: the *encoding* problem and the *vocabulary* problem are different, and only one of them is solved.** Encoding — *my parser meets a field it does not know* — was settled by thirty years of Protobuf/Avro/Thrift, and **we are in good shape and in an unusual position**: not tag-based and not schema-resolution but **open string-keyed maps whose type is itself a content-addressed resolvable entity**, which is Avro's model with the registry problem dissolved. Three places we are ahead: unknown-field preservation is a **correctness** requirement here rather than a courtesy (dropping one changes the content hash, and `ENTITY-NATIVE-TYPE-SYSTEM` §2.4 inverts the field's priority order outright — *"a peer that strips unknown fields is broken; a peer that doesn't validate types is merely Level 0"*); `ENTITY-CBOR-ENCODING` §5.4's **property/mechanism split with a testable precondition** is exactly the Thrift-drops-unknowns hazard, correctly gated, and the field has no equivalent; and null-vs-absent is hash-distinguishing, so we cannot paper it over the way Avro's null-union does. **The `MUST-ignore` literature yields one checkable invariant** — *safe only when ignorable elements cannot carry state-changing semantics, plus a resource bound* — **and we hold two of three**, with the bound unestablished and filed rather than claimed. **§3 is the defect this read found**: unknown-field preservation is stated in **four normative homes at two strengths**, and `ENTITY-CBOR-ENCODING` §5.4 — *which declares itself the canonical home of the entity-fidelity contract* — carries the **SHOULD**, while the correctness argument lives in `ENTITY-CORE-PROTOCOL` §2.10 under a **MUST**;
